Gustavo.Calibration.ComponentPlan — Type
ComponentPlan

The term, resolved segmentation, and θ location for one gain component. range is the component's contiguous span in θ and shape the sizes of its column-major leaf by axis, (; param, node, Frequency, Ti, AntennaName), so reshape(view(θ, range), Tuple(shape)) recovers the leaf. The forward map reads tseg_id[ti]/fseg_id[c] to place a cell in its segment, xf/xt for the term's coordinates, and nchan_seg for the term's per-segment arity, then addresses the leaf block at (node, fs, ts, ant) with node = _feed_node(tying, feed). A block holds the parameters param_shapes(term, nchan_seg[fs]) declares, in declaration order.

Those four tables are indexed by position on the solve GRID. tseg/fseg are the segmentations they resolve, so the identical tables can be rebuilt for data sampled anywhere by placing each foreign sample in its segment (time_segment_ids / freq_segment_ids) — the basis for applying a solution to a different time or channel sampling. fseg is stored resolved, so a data-dependent segmentation never reaches a plan. fstate/tstate are the term's own coordinate constants, resolved once against the solve geometry (freq_coord_state), nothing on an axis the term does not declare. priors[a] is the resolve_priord prior of the plan's a-th station (the leaf's :AntennaName position): stations share a plan whatever their priors. The forward map does not read it. path is the component's name in the model, (:phase, …) or (:logamp, …); the station groups of one name share it.

Gustavo.Calibration.ConstrainedWLS — Type
ConstrainedWLS(A, inv_variances, C, d = nothing)
ConstrainedWLS{T}(A, inv_variances, C, d = nothing)

The weighted least-squares problem min_x ‖diag(√inv_variances)(Ax − b)‖² subject to Cx = d (Cx = 0 when d is nothing or zero), factored once for any number of right-hand sides: F(b) returns x.

The constraints hold exactly: with Cᵀ = [Q₁ Q₂]R, every feasible x is x₀ + Q₂z for the particular solution x₀ = Q₁R⁻ᵀd, and z solves the unconstrained problem in A·Q₂ (a FactoredWLS) against b − Ax₀. Throws if C has dependent rows or A does not determine x on the constraints' null space. T defaults to the common type of A, the weights, C and d.

Gustavo.Calibration.WLSEstimator — Type

A weighted-least-squares estimator reified as a value: observation_model is a callable that, given whatever domain-specific data a fit needs, builds and returns (A, b, inv_variances); penalty regularizes that system — nothing (unregularized), a per-column ridge vector, an arbitrary penalty matrix, or a function of A computing one of those (for penalties whose shape depends on the system size, e.g. one ridge entry per column).

Gustavo.Calibration.unwrap_phase_track — Method
unwrap_phase_track(phases; weights=nothing) -> Vector

Unwrap a phase track to remove ±2π discontinuities between adjacent finite samples, moving samples only by whole multiples of 2π: no trend is estimated, removed, or restored. The walk seeds from argmax of the finite weights when weights is given, else the first finite phase.

A step is resolved only while the true increment plus its noise stays inside ±π, so a track carrying a steep trend (a group delay along frequency, a fringe rate along time) is walked onto the wrong branch systematically — fit the trend out first. Noise puts individual steps over the boundary at random, accumulating 2π errors that a subsequent smooth fit reports as a large trend.

The seed is an algorithmic anchor only; downstream gauge code should fix the phase gauge itself rather than rely on the unwrap reference.

Gustavo.Calibration.weighted_regularized_least_squares — Method
weighted_regularized_least_squares(A, b, inv_variances, penalties)

Ridge-regularized WLS: solve min_x ‖diag(√inv_variances)(Ax - b)‖² + ‖Rx‖² by stacking R onto the weighted design matrix (Rx = 0 as extra zero-target rows) and delegating to weighted_least_squares.

penalties is either a vector of per-column ridge weights λᵢ ≥ 0 (the diagonal case, giving R = Diagonal(√λ)) or an arbitrary penalty matrix R with size(R, 2) == size(A, 2) — e.g. a (scaled) difference operator for roughness penalties. A vector of all-nonpositive entries (no columns penalized) short-circuits to the unregularized solve.

Gustavo.Fring.CARMAModel — Type
CARMAModel{P}(ar, ma, σ2)

The stationary CARMA process a(D)x = b(D)ε along a coordinate, with a(s) = Π(s − r) over the P complex roots ar and b(s) = Π(1 − s/r) over the fewer roots ma (conjugate pairs together, every real part negative), and stationary variance σ2. Its state is the observer canonical one, whose first entry is x: s′ = F·s + g·ε with F the companion matrix of a down its first column and g the coefficients of b. It starts from its stationary distribution.

Gustavo.Fring.Detection — Type
Detection{T} = @NamedTuple{delay, rate, phase, amp, snr, pfa, valid}

The result of a single-baseline fringe search. delay (s) and rate (Hz) are the station-pair group delay and fringe rate; phase (rad) is the constant phase φ referenced to (f0, t0); amp is the coherent amplitude; snr the detection signal-to-noise.

pfa is the probability that noise alone would produce a peak this strong somewhere in the search family the measurement belongs to — the whole family of (baseline × product × scan) searches sharing one false-alarm budget, not this one search in isolation, so it is directly comparable to Stationization.pfa_max.

valid says a peak was measured here, not that it passed any threshold: it is false only for a cell with no usable data (zero total weight, no peak), whose other fields are all zero and carry no information. pfa is the quantity that separates a real fringe from noise.

T is the search's compute precision (real(C) for a ComplexF32/ComplexF64 visibility block).

Gustavo.Fring.FringeWorkspace — Type
FringeWorkspace(::Type{C})

Reusable scratch for baseline_fringe_search at compute type C (ComplexF32 or ComplexF64, matching a visibility block's own eltype): the zero-padded gridding buffer G and the FFT output D, lazily (re)allocated when the padded grid size changes, and the weighted plane a cell is gathered into (_weigh_plane!), resized to it. Pass one per task to avoid allocating the grid (tens of MB where oversample resolves high) on every call — the search runs thousands of times per solve, so reuse removes essentially all of its allocation/GC churn. The FFT plan is not here: it is a pure function of the grid size (and C), so the search-grid geometry carries it (built once per scan, shared read-only across the workspaces that execute it).

Gustavo.Fring.ProfilePreconditioner — Type
ProfilePreconditioner(F, Z, U)

A balancing preconditioner for the profiled curvature A: the inverse of the held-S quadratic K (its factor F) on the complement of the coarse space Z, and A itself on Z,

M⁻¹ = (I − Z E⁻¹ Uᵀ) K⁻¹ (I − U E⁻¹ Zᵀ) + Z E⁻¹ Zᵀ,   E = Zᵀ U,

with U = A·Z. Z holds the constant of each track whose prior holds it: S absorbs those constants, so A sees them through the priors alone while K sees them through every correlation of the track. F and U are read where they are, so a refactorization of F and a new U in place carry over once E is refactored (_refactor_coarse!).

Gustavo.Fring.ProfiledCurvature — Type
ProfiledCurvature(K, profile)

The Hessian of the profiled objective over a system's unknowns: K, the held-S Gauss–Newton quadratic with the priors (and any damping), plus the profile's correction on the parameters, which are the first unknowns (_profile_product!). Applied matrix-free, one pass over the cells per product; its entries are not stored, and indexing it throws. A later step's profile replaces profile in place (_renew!).

Gustavo.Fring.SelectedInverse — Type
SelectedInverse(F, K)

The entries of K⁻¹ on K's sparsity pattern, from its Cholesky factor F by selected inversion (SelectedInversion.jl; the recursion of Takahashi, Fagan & Chen 1973), called as Σ(i, j). _bandpass_track_report(phase_status, amp_status; phase_priors = nothing,
                      amp_priors = nothing) -> NamedTuple

Gustavo.Fring._branch_filter — Method
_branch_filter(model, θ, r, x; mean = nothing) -> (; histories, loglik, best)

Choose the 2π branch of each sample of the phase track θ (radians, on any branch), observed with variances r at the coordinates x under the state-space model, by a Gaussian-sum Kalman filter over branch histories. A sample with non-finite θ_k or r_k not positive and finite is missing. With mean, one row of regressors per sample (SVectors), the track is a linear model in them with flat unknown coefficients plus model's zero-mean process (model must have a proper start), and the coefficients are integrated out; until the samples determine them, each sample takes the branch nearest the track's prediction.

histories holds every history carried to the end as (; lift, weight), most probable first: lift is θ on that history's branches (NaN where missing) and weight its posterior probability among them. loglik is the log marginal likelihood of the wrapped track, summed over those histories, and best the log marginal likelihood of the first history's lift alone, as _pooled_loglik computes it for a single track.

A history branches at a sample over every 2π branch within nsig predictive standard deviations of its prediction, and always the nearest. A history more than prune below the best in log weight is dropped and at most maxhyp are carried. Two histories whose predictions agree to merge_tol of their standard deviations predict the same from there on, so they are merged: weights summed, the more probable one's branches kept. A flat start's first statedim(model) usable samples, which fix its state, each take the branch nearest the previous one. For samples on consecutive points of an evenly spaced grid that loses nothing, since any other choice differs by a polynomial of degree below statedim(model) with values in 2πℤ on the grid, which the flat start absorbs.

Gustavo.Fring._closure_em! — Method
_closure_em!(rows, rw, w, nodes, gauge, kind, chains, priors, width, opts)

EM over a kind's station values x and the parameters of its noise and prior models, from the weights w of the starting solve. Under OutlierMixture, a row's inlier variance is f = σ² + s_c²: its noise variance σ² = 1/rw and its class's floor s_c². Each cycle's E-step gives each row's probability γ of being unexplained, from its residual e against x: π_c/W (W its search's width) against (1 − π_c)·N(e; 0, f); then the Gaussian posterior of x, x̂ and Σ, with each row weighted by (1 − γ)/f. The M-step sets π_c to the mean γ of its class and s_c² to the mean over the class, weighted by 1 − γ, of the floor's posterior second moment, s²(f − s²)/f + (s²/f)²(e² + v) with e against x̂ and v the variance of the row's fitted value (Henderson's variance-component step); and each chain whose prior gives hyperparameters as densities to the maximum of its expected log prior plus hyperprior (_m_step). A row more likely unexplained than not no longer joins stations into a fringe group, as a detection above pfa_max does not; other losses keep reweighting as their IRLS does.

Gustavo.Fring._estimate_hypers — Method
_estimate_hypers(prior, ys, ws, xs; mean = nothing) -> prior

prior with every hyperparameter fixed, estimated from the blocks (ys[i], ws[i], xs[i]). A hyperparameter given as a number is kept; one given as a hyperprior is estimated by type-II MAP over the blocks together, on their marginal likelihood: zero-mean, or with mean integrated out under a flat prior, given as one level group id per block or one design per block (_level_design). A walk without an init leaves its own level free, so it takes no mean; its likelihood is the restricted one (_random_walk_loglik).

Blocks without data (for a walk without an init of order m, with fewer than m usable segments) are ignored; with none left the hyperpriors cannot be resolved and this throws.

Gustavo.Fring._fit_hypers! — Method
_fit_hypers!(priors, x, ctx, solved; tolerance, hyper_tolerance, max_iterations, max_hyper_iterations,
             linsolve, optimizer, warmup = 5) -> (; solved, converged, evaluations, unconverged, remaining)

The hyperparameters the priors give as densities at their type-II MAP, written into priors with the gains x at their MAP under them. The evidence can have more than one maximum along a track's hyperparameters (a smooth track, or one held nearly rigid at the sample spacing), so warmup EM maps (_em_step!, each after the MAP at the last) first move each track toward what its posterior moments support. From there optimizer, an Optimization.jl optimizer that takes a gradient and bounds, maximizes the Laplace log evidence (_hyper_objective) over the log hyperparameters within their support (_log_hyper_boxes), each scaled by the square root of its curvature there, so that one unit of each is about one standard error.

Each evaluation is the MAP of the gains (_profiled_map!, warm from the last, with the exact Hessian) and the evidence and its gradient there. The fit stops once the evidence a Newton step would still gain (remaining, in nats), per track ½ gᵀH⁻¹g over its gradient g projected onto the support and the complete data's curvature H, is below hyper_tolerance; when the optimizer stops on its own; or after max_hyper_iterations iterations, the EM maps among them, where converged is false. solved is the MAP at the starting hyperparameters, and the result's the MAP at the final ones; unconverged counts the MAPs that stopped at max_iterations.

source
Gustavo.Fring._hyper_objective — Method
_hyper_objective(priors, x, ctx, solved, est; linsolve, abstol) -> (L, ∇L, H)

The log posterior density L of the hyperparameters of priors, up to a constant, by Laplace's method at the MAP x that solved (_profiled_map!) reports, and its gradient over the log hyperparameters of the tracks est (_estimated). L = −O − N − ½ log det A + log p(ψ), with O the MAP objective (χ²/2 with S at its optimum plus the prior energy), N the priors' log normalizer (_prior_log_normalizer), A the curvature of the profiled objective in its Gauss–Newton form (_joint_curvature!), and p(ψ) the hyperpriors over the log hyperparameters. Its gradient is in two parts. With L's other terms held at the MAP, the Fisher identity's: each track's expected log prior density under the Gaussian posterior there, covariance A⁻¹, plus its hyperprior, differentiated in its own log hyperparameters (_hyper_gradient). Then the MAP moving with ψ, which changes log det A through the cells' weights: with Λ the observables' summed log det A, dz*/dψ = −H⁻¹ ∂²O/∂z∂ψ gives ½ ∂/∂ψ (aᵀ ∇z E) with a = H⁻¹ ∇z Λ, H the exact Hessian of the profiled objective (_hessian_solve, by linsolve to abstol) and E the prior energy (_logdet_gain_gradient, _track_gradient_dot). H holds per track of est the negative Hessian of its expected log prior plus hyperprior (the M-step's objective) in its own log hyperparameters, the complete data's information about them, at least the evidence's own. A curvature that cannot be factored is an error.

Gustavo.Fring._joint_step — Method
_joint_step(φ, ℓ, λ, linsolve; abstol, z0, state) -> (; z, x′, decrease, iterations)

_profiled_step over both observables at once, with the exact Hessian's cross block between them (JointCurvature): φ and ℓ are the observables' systems (_profiled_systems), λ their dampings and z0 their unknowns at the current point, as (; phase, amp). Gustavo.Fring._meet_phase_gauge! — Method
_meet_phase_gauge!(p, tracks, priors, gauge, cells, sg, components)

Move the phase parameters p.θ onto gauge along the freedom the data leave to the components that span scans (p.shared, every component but the PerScan ones). Their part of a station gain is its shared value; station gains with the same shared parameters share one. On each connected group g of shared values joined by weighted correlations, a phase c_g added to every one of them cancels from every correlation. The move δ, on the shared parameters only, is the one of least prior energy at θ + δ among those that change each shared value by its group's c_g, J_s·δ = Σ_g c_g·1_g, and meet the gauge's constraints on the shared values, C·J_s·(θ + δ) = d (gauge_constraints, one freedom per group). A group joined to a station gain without a shared part has no such freedom and keeps its values. The per-scan components and station gains without data are left to the priors. The gauge's constraints must be reachable by such a move; a model that cannot shift a group's phase is an error.

Gustavo.Fring._posterior_system — Method
_posterior_system(H, b, J, θ, tracks, key; ridge = [])

The MAP problem of one observable with the χ² to second order in its station-gain values x = J·θ (data curvature H and gradient b there) and the exact prior energy of each of tracks, over the parameters θ and the tracks' latent states: the data's quadratic JᵀHJ, each track's state chains (_track_chains!) and w·θ_j²/2 for each pair j => w of ridge, the priors added by _assemble!. key gives each parameter its coordinate along its chain, by which the unknowns are ordered for factoring: the system is then banded, coupling stations only at one frequency and frequencies only along a chain. The data's quadratic keeps every product of stored entries of Jᵀ, H and J, so the pattern does not depend on their values, and _set_data! rewrites it for new values on H's pattern. The system also holds the coarse space of its tracks' constants (_coarse_basis) and buffers for the steps on it.

Gustavo.Fring._profiled_map! — Method
_profiled_map!(x, ctx, priors; tolerance, max_iterations, linsolve, μ = 1e-3, exact = false) -> NamedTuple

The MAP of the parameters ctx.params under the resolved priors, with the station gains x = J·θ ((; phase, amp) values) kept with them, both updated in place, by Levenberg–Marquardt on an objective over the parameters and the tracks' latent unknowns: χ²/2 with S at its optimum plus the tracks' prior energy at those unknowns (_prior_energy). The latent unknowns start at their optimum given the parameters (_current_unknowns) and move with each step, so the energies compared are sums of whitened innovations at given states; re-minimizing over the states for each trial would perturb them by the rounding of a solve as stiff as the stiffest prior. Each step is _profiled_step, damped by μ times each parameter's curvature, with μ following how well the step's model predicted the objective's decrease (Madsen, Nielsen & Tingleff 2004, §3.2). The steps take the Gauss–Newton curvature until one moves no gain by more than one standard error, or by fewer than ten while the objective falls by more than 1.5 times the model's prediction, and the exact Hessian from then on; with exact, a start already in the mode's basin, from the first step. The solve stops once the undamped step moves no gain by tolerance of its standard error from the data, or once no damping lowers the objective. The undamped step is checked after a step that moves no gain by tolerance or lowers the objective by less than tolerance²/2.

Returns (; converged, stalled, change, worst, iterations, krylov, μ, objective, weights): the largest weighted change of the last step, undamped where no damping lowered the objective, and its station gain, the steps and Krylov iterations taken, the damping of the last accepted step, the final objective, and each gain's data weight at the last linearization, the inverse square of its standard error.

Gustavo.Fring._profiled_step — Method
_profiled_step(s, λ, linsolve; abstol, z0 = _current_unknowns(s.sys)) -> (; z, x′, decrease, iterations)

One damped Newton step of the profiled objective for the system s of one observable (_profiled_systems): the minimum over its unknowns of the held-S quadratic plus the profile's correction (exact or Gauss–Newton, as profile holds), plus λ/2·Σ d_j(θ′_j − θ_j)² over the parameters, d their curvatures (_reduce!). It is solved by linsolve, a Krylov method of LinearSolve.jl, preconditioned by ProfilePreconditioner over the constants of its tracks, from the current point z0, by default the parameters with the latent unknowns at their optimum given them (_current_unknowns). There the initial residual is the objective's negative gradient (_step_residual), and the solve stops once the residual in the preconditioned norm, which measures the error in the step's own standard errors, falls below abstol or a tenth of its start (an inexact Newton step; Eisenstat & Walker 1996). Returns the unknowns, the new gain values (in a buffer of the system the next step overwrites), the decrease the undamped model predicts and the Krylov iterations taken. The decrease is −gᵀδ − δᵀ(A − λ)δ/2 from the gradient g and the increment δ: the model's own values are differences of terms of the order of the stiffest prior's curvature, and lose the decrease to rounding.

Gustavo.Fring._resolve_oversample — Method
_resolve_oversample(oversample, fax, nchan) -> Int

The zero-padding factor to build the search grid at: an explicit positive Int passes through, :auto reads it off the frequency axis.

The padded grid's delay cell is 1/(oversample·n·Δf) and the main lobe of the synthesized delay response is about 1/(n·Δf) wide, so oversample is the number of cells laid across the main lobe. One cell never suffices: the search reports whichever cell is highest, and the sampled peak is scalloped away from the true one by up to half a cell.

How finely the lobe must be sampled depends on what it is competing against. Contiguous channels put a single lobe in the delay window, and scalloping costs only accuracy — which _polish_peak_exact! then recovers off-grid. Channels gathered into separated bands put a comb of alias lobes beside it, spaced by the reciprocal of the band-origin spacing, and a scalloped main lobe can be reported below one of them; the polish cannot undo that, because it refines whichever lobe it was seeded in. The comb's density tracks sparsity — the common grid's size over the real channel count, the same quantity _resolve_algorithm reads to hand a very gapped axis to HierarchicalMBD — so the sampling requirement tracks it too.

The thresholds come from injected fringes scored on how often the recovered delay lands on the true lobe, swept over band sparsity and signal-to-noise: 2 cells hold to sparsity ≈ 1.5; 4 is indistinguishable from 8 up to sparsity ≈ 3; only at the HierarchicalMBD crossover (sparsity 4) does 8 measurably beat 4, and by 1–3 points. Below 2 cells the identification collapses — to 20–50% — at every signal-to-noise, because it is a grid failure rather than a noise one.

:auto is a pure function of the frequency axis, so a recorded FringeSearch still determines the grid: replaying it on the same data resolves identically. HierarchicalMBD floors its own band and band-center grids at 4 regardless, and takes only its rate axis from this.

Gustavo.Fring.kalman_filter — Method
kalman_filter(model, y, r, x) -> NamedTuple

Forward Kalman filter of the state-space model observed as y_k = s_k[1] + ε_k, ε_k ~ N(0, r_k), at the coordinates x (any spacing, either direction; only the steps x_k − x_{k−1} enter). A sample with non-finite y_k or r_k not positive and finite is missing: predicted through, not updated.

A flat start (RandomWalkModel) is filtered in information form until the observed samples determine the state, then in covariance form; this is exact. It throws if the samples never determine the state.

Fields of the result, per sample k:

  • μf, Pf: filtered mean and covariance, for k > ndiffuse;
  • ηf, Λf: filtered information vector and matrix, for k ≤ ndiffuse;
  • μp, Pp: predicted mean and covariance, for k > ndiffuse + 1, and for k = 1 with a proper start;
  • A, Q: the transition into sample k (k ≥ 2);
  • v, S: innovation and its variance (NaN and 0 where not observed or k ≤ ndiffuse);
  • loglik: the log marginal likelihood of the observed samples, restricted (the flat start integrated out under the Lebesgue measure on the first state) for a flat start.

Gustavo.Fring.kalman_mv_filter — Method
kalman_mv_filter(pairs, ys, rs, times, models) -> (xf, Pf, xp, Pp, As, loglik)

Forward Kalman filter over the stacked states of models, one state-space model per station, each with a proper start. At step k, observation j is a difference of two stations' values (the first entry of each station's state),

ys[k][j] = θ[a] − θ[b] + ε,   ε ~ N(0, rs[k][j])

with (a, b) = pairs[j], the same station pair at every step; ys[k] and rs[k] share pairs' indices. An observation whose value is not finite or whose variance is not positive is skipped, so a step observes any subset.

Rows are applied as sequential scalar updates (diagonal R, so this is exact and avoids an m×m inverse); a row has two nonzero design entries, so an update costs O(n²) for n stacked states.

Returns the filtered and predicted means as n × nsteps matrices, their covariances and the transitions into each step as n × n × nsteps arrays, and the joint log marginal likelihood — so step k is view(xf, :, k) / view(Pf, :, :, k). Station i's value is state 1 + sum(statedim, models[1:i-1]).

Gustavo.Fring.search_scan — Method
search_scan(group::XRadio.ProcessingSet, geom::DataGeometry, params::FringeSearch;
            executor = SerialScheduler(), t0 = geom.t0) -> DimStack

Fringe-search every cross (antenna pair, feed pair) cell of a scan group, one Measurement Set per spectral window sharing one time axis. Each cell's plane is gathered across the members in frequency order, so the delay search spans every window. Autocorrelations are skipped. geom supplies the reference frequency f0, the default phase epoch, and the order of the antenna pairs.

Each cell's pfa is computed over the scan's family of ncross×npol searches, which share one budget (Bonferroni), so a recorded pfa is directly comparable to Stationization.pfa_max and does not depend on how many other scans a solve holds.

t0 (seconds) is the epoch the detection phases are referenced to (delay/rate/SNR are epoch-invariant). Quoting a phase a lever arm from the data costs it 2π·σ_rate·Δt, so the scan midpoint is usually wanted. The default is geom's track epoch, which is the midpoint only for a single-scan geometry.

Returns a DimStack over AntennaPair × FeedPair whose layers are the seven Detection fields (:delay/:rate/:phase/:amp/:snr/:pfa/:valid), so one cell reads back as a Detection NamedTuple. The AntennaPair lookup holds each cross pair's antenna names, in geom's station order, and the FeedPair lookup the feed-index pairs. The layers' element type is the real type of the visibilities. Results are bit-identical to the serial loop regardless of the fan-out executor.

Gustavo.Fring.solve_antenna_systems! — Method
solve_antenna_systems!(θ, scans, components, antennas; gauge, opts, times) -> (ncomp, covered, unlinked, fits)

Solve the stage-B fringe systems (delay and rate) over scans and accumulate the per-(station, feed) values into θ at the columns the model declares. antennas are the station names the layout numbers; each detection's antenna pair is matched to them by name. components is a vector of (plan::ComponentPlan, kind::Symbol) with kind ∈ (:delay, :rate). Multiple components of the same kind are summed per (station, feed) observation: e.g. a feed-common PerScan × SharedFeeds term plus a GlobalTime × ExceptFeed(1) inter-feed offset both feed the delay system, so a row on feed k ≠ 1 touches both columns and a stable inter-feed offset is solved once across the track (bright scans pin it; weak scans inherit it, tying feeds that would otherwise split). Returns the delay system's component count and covered — the (antenna name, feed, scan index) triples the solve CONSTRAINS in every solved kind: those an accepted detection (pfa ≤ pfa_max) touches on that feed. Coverage is independent of the gauge (see Stationization for how inconsistent rows are weighted). A θ column no accepted detection touches — per-scan or scan-spanning, per-feed or shared by every feed — is set to zero (identity gain), as is one under OutlierMixture that only detections it leaves unexplained touch. With a single per-scan/per-feed component per kind and one scan, each scan's system is independent and solves exactly as it would alone.

A component with a prior along Ti adds the prior's rows over its time segments, placed at the mean of times, the solve geometry's sample times, in each segment. The prior ties the segments' values, so a column it ties counts as constrained, and a station's feed is covered where every column of its value is constrained and one is tied.

fits holds, per kind that ran the EM of Stationization, the resolved prior of each chain, the OutlierMixture parameters per row class, and the rows more likely unexplained than not.

unlinked holds the (kind, scan index) pairs in which the accepted detections leave the offset between some station's feeds free: no accepted detection relates different feeds of that fringe group. The gauge then sets it, at the gauge's preferred station, so it is not measured.

Gustavo.Fring.solve_joint_gains! — Method
solve_joint_gains!(components, data, geom::DataGeometry; cover, gauge, kw...) -> NamedTuple

Fit the parameters of the fitted components ((; phase, logamp), each a vector of components, each the station blocks antenna_blocks returns) to the weighted sums data = (; r, w): r = Σ w·v and w = Σ w over (Scan, AntennaPair, FeedPair, cell), entry (si, c) covering the time and channel cover[si, c] = (t, ch) of the plans. Writes the parameters into the blocks' θ.

Every station's prior on every component must be an IIDPrior or a state-space prior along one axis whose start is proper (OUPrior, CARMAPrior, or RandomWalkPrior with an init); a state-space prior must act on parameters that each enter their station gains with coefficient one, and at most one component per (station, observable) carries one along each axis.

The model is V = g_a·S·conj(g_b) with g = exp(ℓ + iφ) the station gains and S per scan, station pair and feed pair profiled out. The start estimates the station gains alone (rounds of each gain's estimate given the others), puts each phase chain on its most probable 2π branches (a sample that several station gains share, as a per-scan component splits them, at their mean), and solves for the parameters nearest those estimates under the priors. The MAP is found by Levenberg–Marquardt with the damping scaled by each parameter's curvature, and hyperparameters given as densities at their type-II MAP, by hyper_optimizer (an Optimization.jl optimizer that takes a gradient and bounds; L-BFGS-B by default) on their Laplace evidence within their support (_fit_hypers!), stopping once the evidence a Newton step would still gain is below hyper_tolerance nats or after max_hyper_iterations iterations. The gauge then moves the phases along the freedoms the data leave, at least cost in prior energy (_meet_phase_gauge!).

Returns (; phase, amp, gains, converged, stalled): per observable one (; path, block, station, node, across, axis, windows, prior, pieces, values) per chain: its component's path, station block and position there, feed node and segment of the other axis, its axis, the piece (spectral window or scan) of each of its pieces of samples, its prior with the hyperparameters resolved, and its samples' station-gain values; gains, the StationGains sg and every station gain's final phase and amp values; and how the MAP solve ended. A station-gain value is NaN where the gain has no data. A chain whose station gains carry no data, and any other parameter that reads none, is not solved and keeps its value.

Gustavo.Fring.steer_scan — Method
steer_scan(group, geom, res, f0, t0, sta_delay, sta_rate; cells, executor = SerialScheduler())

Re-measure every cell of res, the search_scan result for scan group group, at the delay and rate the station solution predicts for it (τ_{a,fa} − τ_{b,fb}, and the same difference in rate) rather than at a blind search peak. sta_delay and sta_rate are DimArrays over AntennaName × Feed, as scan_antenna_terms returns them; the result is labeled like res.
