Gustavo.Calibration.ComponentPlan — Type
ComponentPlan

The term, resolved segmentation, and θ location for one gain component. range is the component's contiguous span in θ and shape the sizes of its column-major leaf by axis, (; param, node, Frequency, Ti, AntennaName), so reshape(view(θ, range), Tuple(shape)) recovers the leaf. The forward map reads tseg_id[ti]/fseg_id[c] to place a cell in its segment, xf/xt for the term's coordinates, and nchan_seg for the term's per-segment arity, then addresses the leaf block at (node, fs, ts, ant) with node = _feed_node(tying, feed). A block holds the parameters param_shapes(term, nchan_seg[fs]) declares, in declaration order.

Those four tables are indexed by position on the solve GRID. tseg/fseg are the segmentations they resolve, so the identical tables can be rebuilt for data sampled anywhere by placing each foreign sample in its segment (time_segment_ids / freq_segment_ids) — the basis for applying a solution to a different time or channel sampling. fseg is stored resolved, so a data-dependent segmentation never reaches a plan. fstate/tstate are the term's own coordinate constants, resolved once against the solve geometry (freq_coord_state), nothing on an axis the term does not declare. priors[a] is the resolve_priord prior of the plan's a-th station (the leaf's :AntennaName position): stations share a plan whatever their priors. The forward map does not read it. path is the component's name in the model, (:phase, …) or (:logamp, …); the station groups of one name share it.

Gustavo.Calibration.ConstrainedWLS — Type
ConstrainedWLS(A, inv_variances, C, d = nothing)
ConstrainedWLS{T}(A, inv_variances, C, d = nothing)

The weighted least-squares problem min_x ‖diag(√inv_variances)(Ax − b)‖² subject to Cx = d (Cx = 0 when d is nothing or zero), factored once for any number of right-hand sides: F(b) returns x.

The constraints hold exactly: with Cᵀ = [Q₁ Q₂]R, every feasible x is x₀ + Q₂z for the particular solution x₀ = Q₁R⁻ᵀd, and z solves the unconstrained problem in A·Q₂ (a FactoredWLS) against b − Ax₀. Throws if C has dependent rows or A does not determine x on the constraints' null space. T defaults to the common type of A, the weights, C and d.

Gustavo.Calibration.WLSEstimator — Type
WLSEstimator(observation_model, penalty = nothing)

A weighted-least-squares estimator reified as a value: observation_model is a callable that, given whatever domain-specific data a fit needs, builds and returns (A, b, inv_variances); penalty regularizes that system — nothing (unregularized), a per-column ridge vector, an arbitrary penalty matrix, or a function of A computing one of those (for penalties whose shape depends on the system size, e.g. one ridge entry per column).

Gustavo.Calibration.unwrap_phase_track — Method
unwrap_phase_track(phases; weights=nothing) -> Vector

Unwrap a phase track to remove ±2π discontinuities between adjacent finite samples, moving samples only by whole multiples of 2π: no trend is estimated, removed, or restored. The walk seeds from argmax of the finite weights when weights is given, else the first finite phase.

A step is resolved only while the true increment plus its noise stays inside ±π, so a track carrying a steep trend (a group delay along frequency, a fringe rate along time) is walked onto the wrong branch systematically — fit the trend out first. Noise puts individual steps over the boundary at random, accumulating 2π errors that a subsequent smooth fit reports as a large trend.

The seed is an algorithmic anchor only; downstream gauge code should fix the phase gauge itself rather than rely on the unwrap reference.

Gustavo.Calibration.weighted_regularized_least_squares — Method
weighted_regularized_least_squares(A, b, inv_variances, penalties)

Ridge-regularized WLS: solve min_x ‖diag(√inv_variances)(Ax - b)‖² + ‖Rx‖² by stacking R onto the weighted design matrix (Rx = 0 as extra zero-target rows) and delegating to weighted_least_squares.

penalties is either a vector of per-column ridge weights λᵢ ≥ 0 (the diagonal case, giving R = Diagonal(√λ)) or an arbitrary penalty matrix R with size(R, 2) == size(A, 2) — e.g. a (scaled) difference operator for roughness penalties. A vector of all-nonpositive entries (no columns penalized) short-circuits to the unregularized solve.

Gustavo.Fring.CARMAModel — Type
CARMAModel{P}(ar, ma, σ2)

The stationary CARMA process a(D)x = b(D)ε along a coordinate, with a(s) = Π(s − r) over the P complex roots ar and b(s) = Π(1 − s/r) over the fewer roots ma (conjugate pairs together, every real part negative), and stationary variance σ2. Its state is the observer canonical one, whose first entry is x: s′ = F·s + g·ε with F the companion matrix of a down its first column and g the coefficients of b. It starts from its stationary distribution.

Gustavo.Fring.Detection — Type
Detection{T} = @NamedTuple{delay, rate, phase, amp, snr, pfa, valid}

The result of a single-baseline fringe search. delay (s) and rate (Hz) are the station-pair group delay and fringe rate; phase (rad) is the constant phase φ referenced to (f0, t0); amp is the coherent amplitude; snr the detection signal-to-noise.

pfa is the probability that noise alone would produce a peak this strong somewhere in the search family the measurement belongs to — the whole family of (baseline × product × scan) searches sharing one false-alarm budget, not this one search in isolation, so it is directly comparable to Stationization.pfa_max.

valid says a peak was measured here, not that it passed any threshold: it is false only for a cell with no usable data (zero total weight, no peak), whose other fields are all zero and carry no information. pfa is the quantity that separates a real fringe from noise.

T is the search's compute precision (real(C) for a ComplexF32/ComplexF64 visibility block).

Gustavo.Fring.FringeWorkspace — Type
FringeWorkspace(::Type{C})

Reusable scratch for baseline_fringe_search at compute type C (ComplexF32 or ComplexF64, matching a visibility block's own eltype): the zero-padded gridding buffer G and the FFT output D, lazily (re)allocated when the padded grid size changes, and the weighted plane a cell is gathered into (_weigh_plane!), resized to it. Pass one per task to avoid allocating the grid (tens of MB where oversample resolves high) on every call — the search runs thousands of times per solve, so reuse removes essentially all of its allocation/GC churn. The FFT plan is not here: it is a pure function of the grid size (and C), so the search-grid geometry carries it (built once per scan, shared read-only across the workspaces that execute it).

Gustavo.Fring.ProfilePreconditioner — Type
ProfilePreconditioner(F, Z, U)

A balancing preconditioner for the profiled curvature A: the inverse of the held-S quadratic K (its factor F) on the complement of the coarse space Z, and A itself on Z,

M⁻¹ = (I − Z E⁻¹ Uᵀ) K⁻¹ (I − U E⁻¹ Zᵀ) + Z E⁻¹ Zᵀ,   E = Zᵀ U,

with U = A·Z. Z holds the constant of each track whose prior holds it: S absorbs those constants, so A sees them through the priors alone while K sees them through every correlation of the track.

Gustavo.Fring.ProfiledCurvature — Type
ProfiledCurvature(K, profile)

The Hessian of the profiled objective over a system's unknowns: K, the held-S Gauss–Newton quadratic with the priors (and any damping), plus the profile's correction on the free slots, which are the first unknowns (_profile_product!). Applied matrix-free, one pass over the cells per product; its entries are not stored, and indexing it throws.

Gustavo.Fring._bandpass_track_report — Method
_bandpass_track_report(phase_status, amp_status; phase_priors = nothing,
                      amp_priors = nothing) -> NamedTuple

Gustavo.Fring._branch_filter — Method
_branch_filter(model, θ, r, x; mean = nothing) -> (; histories, loglik, best)

Choose the 2π branch of each sample of the phase track θ (radians, on any branch), observed with variances r at the coordinates x under the state-space model, by a Gaussian-sum Kalman filter over branch histories. A sample with non-finite θ_k or r_k not positive and finite is missing. With mean, one row of regressors per sample (SVectors), the track is a linear model in them with flat unknown coefficients plus model's zero-mean process (model must have a proper start), and the coefficients are integrated out; until the samples determine them, each sample takes the branch nearest the track's prediction.

histories holds every history carried to the end as (; lift, weight), most probable first: lift is θ on that history's branches (NaN where missing) and weight its posterior probability among them. loglik is the log marginal likelihood of the wrapped track, summed over those histories, and best the log marginal likelihood of the first history's lift alone, as _pooled_loglik computes it for a single track.

A history branches at a sample over every 2π branch within nsig predictive standard deviations of its prediction, and always the nearest. A history more than prune below the best in log weight is dropped and at most maxhyp are carried. Two histories whose predictions agree to merge_tol of their standard deviations predict the same from there on, so they are merged: weights summed, the more probable one's branches kept. A flat start's first statedim(model) usable samples, which fix its state, each take the branch nearest the previous one. For samples on consecutive points of an evenly spaced grid that loses nothing, since any other choice differs by a polynomial of degree below statedim(model) with values in 2πℤ on the grid, which the flat start absorbs.

Gustavo.Fring._closure_em! — Method
_closure_em!(rows, rw, w, nodes, gauge, kind, chains, priors, width, opts)

EM over a kind's station values x and the parameters of its noise and prior models, from the weights w of the starting solve. Under OutlierMixture, a row's inlier variance is f = σ² + s_c²: its noise variance σ² = 1/rw and its class's floor s_c². Each cycle's E-step gives each row's probability γ of being unexplained, from its residual e against x: π_c/W (W its search's width) against (1 − π_c)·N(e; 0, f); then the Gaussian posterior of x, x̂ and Σ, with each row weighted by (1 − γ)/f. The M-step sets π_c to the mean γ of its class and s_c² to the mean over the class, weighted by 1 − γ, of the floor's posterior second moment, s²(f − s²)/f + (s²/f)²(e² + v) with e against x̂ and v the variance of the row's fitted value (Henderson's variance-component step); and each chain whose prior gives hyperparameters as densities to the maximum of its expected log prior plus hyperprior (_m_step). A row more likely unexplained than not no longer joins stations into a fringe group, as a detection above pfa_max does not; other losses keep reweighting as their IRLS does.

Gustavo.Fring._em_hypers! — Method
_em_hypers!(priors, x, ctx; linsolve, tolerance) -> Float64

One EM update of the hyperparameters the acting tracks' priors give as densities, at the MAP gains x: the E-step takes the moments of each track's state chain under the Gaussian posterior at the MAP, with the curvature ProfilePreconditioner inverts (held-S, except on the track constants, which keep their prior variance); the M-step maximizes each track's expected log prior density plus its hyperprior over the log hyperparameters. Writes the new hyperparameters into priors and returns the largest change of a log hyperparameter.

Gustavo.Fring._estimate_hypers — Method
_estimate_hypers(prior, ys, ws, xs; mean = nothing) -> prior

prior with every hyperparameter fixed, estimated from the blocks (ys[i], ws[i], xs[i]). A hyperparameter given as a number is kept; one given as a hyperprior is estimated by type-II MAP over the blocks together, on their marginal likelihood: zero-mean, or with mean integrated out under a flat prior, given as one level group id per block or one design per block (_level_design). A walk without an init leaves its own level free, so it takes no mean; its likelihood is the restricted one (_random_walk_loglik). With branches, the blocks are phases on unknown 2π branches and the likelihood is summed over their branches (_branch_filter).

Blocks without data (for a walk without an init of order m, with fewer than m usable segments) are ignored; with none left the hyperpriors cannot be resolved and this throws.

Gustavo.Fring._joint_bandpass_gauge! — Method
_joint_bandpass_gauge!(gains, data, layout, blocks, gauge, phase_tracks, track_pins) -> (; tracks, pinned, meet)

Fix the phase frame of a joint bandpass solve over every gain slot's phase φ, numbered by _gain_slot_ids: impose gauge and hold the band constant of each phase track. phase_tracks(known) builds the phase tracks (_bandpass_tracks) given the slots with data no pin fixes; they are returned with their holds (_hold_phase_constants!), the held slots marked in track_pins and the pinned ones in pinned and in the pinned layer of gains. A held or pinned slot's amplitude is solved like any other slot's.

Under PinAntenna there is one GaugeFreedom per connected component and the reference's slot in each is pinned at zero: the reference's track is zero wherever it has data, and the priors act on every other track relative to it. In a component where the reference has no data, the next reference with data is pinned (gauge_anchor), and the reference's slot there is filled by its prior. meet is then nothing. Under any other gauge the freedoms are the shifts that neither the data nor the priors of the tracks see (_gauge_directions): the solve pins what the holds leave of them, and afterwards moves along the freedoms onto the constraints C·φ = d the gauge gives (_meet_gauge!). meet is (; rows, d, freedoms): rows[j] the (slot, coefficient) pairs of row j and freedoms[k] the slots of freedom k with their shift.

Only the phase is a gauge freedom. Multiplying the gains of a set of nodes by a shared c sends g_a·S·conj(g_b) to |c|²·g_a·S·conj(g_b) on every correlation internal to that set: the phase of c cancels between the two conjugated factors. The sets on which it cancels everywhere are the connected components of the joint-bandpass graph, so the data leave one phase per component free. A station-uniform segmentation splits the graph along the array-wide (frequency segment, time segment) cells, so the free direction is a phase spectrum common to every station; a model in which one station breaks mid-track keeps the whole track in one component, because the stations that hold one gain over it bridge the broken station's two segments.

A proper prior along frequency is not invariant to that spectrum: it sees all of it but the part its track's mean takes up — a constant under a level, a line in frequency under a delay. A pin settles the spectrum by the reference's data: the reference's bandpass is zero by definition, and its structure appears, sign flipped, on every other track, where their priors act on it. A prior on the reference's own track has nothing left to act on, so a fully pinned track takes no part in the prior fit. Other gauges leave the spectrum to the priors: only the shifts every prior is blind to are freedoms, each fixed by a projection onto its shift being zero (under a zero sum, of every slot). Along such a freedom the data and the priors are unchanged, so the gauge changes no gauge-invariant quantity, nor the posterior of anything the priors see.

The same bridging on the frequency axis makes a pin PARTIAL: where a station holds one gain across cells the others split, those cells lie in one component, and its single pin fixes ONE segment of the pinned station's track while the rest of that track is fitted.

Besides that common phase, each (station, feed, time segment) track has a free band-constant phase and log-amplitude: S is per scan and baseline but frequency-flat, so it absorbs a band-constant factor on any one station, and a prior is profiled over that constant (see the header of bandpasspriors.jl). Pinning |g| as well would assert the reference antenna has a flat amplitude bandpass, discarding structure that is identifiable (mean-removing `log|Vab| = laa + lab + lsabover the band eliminateslsand leaves a signless-Laplacian system, full rank wherever each connected group of baselines has an odd loop; seesplitfreeamplitudes!` for a group without one) and biasing every other station through the inconsistency.

Gustavo.Fring._posterior_system — Method
_posterior_system(H, b, x, free, tracks, key)

The MAP problem of one observable's slot values x with the χ² to second order (data curvature H and gradient b at x, over every slot) and the exact prior energy of each of tracks, over the free slots (the other slots keep their values), the tracks' latent states and their means: the data's quadratic and each track's state chains (_track_chains!), the priors added by _assemble!. key gives each slot's frequency, by which the unknowns are ordered for factoring: the system is then banded, coupling stations only at one frequency and frequencies only along a track. Gustavo.Fring._profiled_map! — Method
_profiled_map!(x, ctx, priors; tolerance, max_iterations, linsolve, μ = 1e-3, exact = false) -> NamedTuple

The MAP of the gains x ((; phase, amp) slot values, updated in place) under the resolved priors, by Levenberg–Marquardt on an objective over the slots and the tracks' latent unknowns: χ²/2 with S at its optimum plus the acting tracks' prior energy at those unknowns (_prior_energy). The latent unknowns start at their optimum given the slots (_current_unknowns) and move with each step, so the energies compared are sums of whitened innovations at given states; re-minimizing over the states for each trial would perturb them by the rounding of a solve as stiff as the stiffest prior. Each step is _profiled_step, damped by μ times the largest data curvature of its observable, with μ following how well the step's model predicted the objective's decrease (Madsen, Nielsen & Tingleff 2004, §3.2). The steps take the Gauss–Newton curvature until one moves no slot by more than one standard error, or by fewer than ten while the objective falls by more than 1.5 times the model's prediction, and the exact Hessian from then on; with exact, a start already in the mode's basin, from the first step. The solve stops once the undamped step moves no slot by tolerance of its standard error from the data, or once no damping lowers the objective. The undamped step is checked after a step that moves no slot by tolerance or lowers the objective by less than tolerance²/2.

Returns (; converged, stalled, change, worst, iterations, krylov, μ, objective, weights): the largest weighted change of the last step, undamped where no damping lowered the objective, and its slot, the steps and Krylov iterations taken, the damping of the last accepted step, the final objective, and each slot's data weight at the last linearization, the inverse square of its standard error.

Gustavo.Fring._profiled_step — Method
_profiled_step(s, tracks, λ, linsolve; abstol, z0 = _current_unknowns(s.sys)) -> (; z, x′, decrease, iterations, P, K)

One damped Newton step of the profiled objective for the system s of one observable (_profiled_systems): the minimum over its unknowns of the held-S quadratic plus the profile's correction (exact or Gauss–Newton, as profile holds), plus λ/2·‖x′ − x‖² over the free slots. It is solved by linsolve, a Krylov method of LinearSolve.jl, preconditioned by ProfilePreconditioner over the constants of tracks, from the current point z0, by default the free slots with the latent unknowns at their optimum given them (_current_unknowns). There the initial residual is the objective's negative gradient (_step_residual), and the solve stops once the residual in the preconditioned norm, which measures the error in the step's own standard errors, falls below abstol or a tenth of its start (an inexact Newton step; Eisenstat & Walker 1996). Returns the unknowns, the new slot values, the decrease the undamped model predicts, the Krylov iterations taken, the preconditioner, and the damped system it factors. The decrease is −gᵀδ − δᵀ(A − λ)δ/2 from the gradient g and the increment δ: the model's own values are differences of terms of the order of the stiffest prior's curvature, and lose the decrease to rounding.

Gustavo.Fring._resolve_oversample — Method
_resolve_oversample(oversample, fax, nchan) -> Int

The zero-padding factor to build the search grid at: an explicit positive Int passes through, :auto reads it off the frequency axis.

The padded grid's delay cell is 1/(oversample·n·Δf) and the main lobe of the synthesized delay response is about 1/(n·Δf) wide, so oversample is the number of cells laid across the main lobe. One cell never suffices: the search reports whichever cell is highest, and the sampled peak is scalloped away from the true one by up to half a cell.

How finely the lobe must be sampled depends on what it is competing against. Contiguous channels put a single lobe in the delay window, and scalloping costs only accuracy — which _polish_peak_exact! then recovers off-grid. Channels gathered into separated bands put a comb of alias lobes beside it, spaced by the reciprocal of the band-origin spacing, and a scalloped main lobe can be reported below one of them; the polish cannot undo that, because it refines whichever lobe it was seeded in. The comb's density tracks sparsity — the common grid's size over the real channel count, the same quantity _resolve_algorithm reads to hand a very gapped axis to HierarchicalMBD — so the sampling requirement tracks it too.

The thresholds come from injected fringes scored on how often the recovered delay lands on the true lobe, swept over band sparsity and signal-to-noise: 2 cells hold to sparsity ≈ 1.5; 4 is indistinguishable from 8 up to sparsity ≈ 3; only at the HierarchicalMBD crossover (sparsity 4) does 8 measurably beat 4, and by 1–3 points. Below 2 cells the identification collapses — to 20–50% — at every signal-to-noise, because it is a grid failure rather than a noise one.

:auto is a pure function of the frequency axis, so a recorded FringeSearch still determines the grid: replaying it on the same data resolves identically. HierarchicalMBD floors its own band and band-center grids at 4 regardless, and takes only its rate axis from this.

Gustavo.Fring._squarem! — Method
_squarem!(priors, x, ctx, solved; tolerance, max_iterations, max_em_iterations, linsolve)
    -> (; solved, converged, evaluations, unconverged, moved)

The hyperparameters the priors give as densities by MAP-EM, accelerated by SQUAREM (Varadhan & Roland 2008, scheme S3) over their logs, written into priors with the gains x at their MAP under them. One EM map is the MAP of the gains (_profiled_map!, warm from the last, with the exact Hessian) followed by _em_hypers!. Each cycle takes two EM maps from ψ₀, extrapolates along them by the step α = −‖r‖/‖v‖, bounded by a length that grows after each step it bounds, and takes one EM map from there. EM is slow where the data barely determine a direction, so a plain EM step understates the distance to its fixed point. The cycles stop once one moves no gain by tolerance of its standard error, from the MAP it starts at to the converged MAP at the extrapolated hyperparameters (moved, the largest such change), or after max_em_iterations EM maps: the hyperparameters the gains no longer depend on are left where EM has taken them. solved is the MAP at the starting hyperparameters, and the result's the MAP at the final ones; unconverged counts the EM maps whose MAP stopped at max_iterations.

Gustavo.Fring.kalman_filter — Method
kalman_filter(model, y, r, x) -> NamedTuple

Forward Kalman filter of the state-space model observed as y_k = s_k[1] + ε_k, ε_k ~ N(0, r_k), at the coordinates x (any spacing, either direction; only the steps x_k − x_{k−1} enter). A sample with non-finite y_k or r_k not positive and finite is missing: predicted through, not updated.

A flat start (RandomWalkModel) is filtered in information form until the observed samples determine the state, then in covariance form; this is exact. It throws if the samples never determine the state.

Fields of the result, per sample k:

  • μf, Pf: filtered mean and covariance, for k > ndiffuse;
  • ηf, Λf: filtered information vector and matrix, for k ≤ ndiffuse;
  • μp, Pp: predicted mean and covariance, for k > ndiffuse + 1, and for k = 1 with a proper start;
  • A, Q: the transition into sample k (k ≥ 2);
  • v, S: innovation and its variance (NaN and 0 where not observed or k ≤ ndiffuse);
  • loglik: the log marginal likelihood of the observed samples, restricted (the flat start integrated out under the Lebesgue measure on the first state) for a flat start.

Gustavo.Fring.kalman_mv_filter — Method
kalman_mv_filter(pairs, ys, rs, times, models) -> (xf, Pf, xp, Pp, As, loglik)

Forward Kalman filter over the stacked states of models, one state-space model per station, each with a proper start. At step k, observation j is a difference of two stations' values (the first entry of each station's state),

ys[k][j] = θ[a] − θ[b] + ε,   ε ~ N(0, rs[k][j])

with (a, b) = pairs[j], the same station pair at every step; ys[k] and rs[k] share pairs' indices. An observation whose value is not finite or whose variance is not positive is skipped, so a step observes any subset.

Rows are applied as sequential scalar updates (diagonal R, so this is exact and avoids an m×m inverse); a row has two nonzero design entries, so an update costs O(n²) for n stacked states.

Returns the filtered and predicted means as n × nsteps matrices, their covariances and the transitions into each step as n × n × nsteps arrays, and the joint log marginal likelihood — so step k is view(xf, :, k) / view(Pf, :, :, k). Station i's value is state 1 + sum(statedim, models[1:i-1]).

Gustavo.Fring.solve_antenna_systems! — Method
solve_antenna_systems!(θ, scans, components, antennas; gauge, opts, times) -> (ncomp, covered, unlinked, fits)

Solve the stage-B fringe systems (delay and rate) over scans and accumulate the per-(station, feed) values into θ at the columns the model declares. antennas are the station names the layout numbers; each detection's antenna pair is matched to them by name. components is a vector of (plan::ComponentPlan, kind::Symbol) with kind ∈ (:delay, :rate). Multiple components of the same kind are summed per (station, feed) observation: e.g. a feed-common PerScan × SharedFeeds term plus a GlobalTime × ExceptFeed(1) inter-feed offset both feed the delay system, so a row on feed k ≠ 1 touches both columns and a stable inter-feed offset is solved once across the track (bright scans pin it; weak scans inherit it, tying feeds that would otherwise split). Coverage is independent of the gauge (see Stationization for how inconsistent rows are weighted). A θ column no accepted detection touches — per-scan or scan-spanning, per-feed or shared by every feed — is set to zero (identity gain), as is one under OutlierMixture that only detections it leaves unexplained touch. With a single per-scan/per-feed component per kind and one scan, each scan's system is independent and solves exactly as it would alone.

A component with a prior along Ti adds the prior's rows over its time segments, placed at the mean of times, the solve geometry's sample times, in each segment. The prior ties the segments' values, so a column it ties counts as constrained, and a station's feed is covered where every column of its value is constrained and one is tied.

fits holds, per kind that ran the EM of Stationization, the resolved prior of each chain, the OutlierMixture parameters per row class, and the rows more likely unexplained than not.

unlinked holds the (kind, scan index) pairs in which the accepted detections leave the offset between some station's feeds free: no accepted detection relates different feeds of that fringe group. The gauge then sets it, at the gauge's preferred station, so it is not measured.

Gustavo.Fring.solve_joint_bandpass! — Method
solve_joint_bandpass!(θ, scans, geom::DataGeometry, phase_blocks, amp_blocks;
                      phase_level_blocks = [], amp_level_blocks = [], delay_blocks = [],
                      gauge, max_iterations = 200, max_em_iterations = 100, tolerance = 0.01,
                      flat_span = 0.01, eltype = nothing, tseg = nothing,
                      linsolve = KrylovJL_CG(; linesearch = true),
                      executor = SerialScheduler()) -> (; phase, amp)

Jointly solve the per-(station, feed, frequency segment) complex bandpass gain and a per-scan, per-station-pair, per-feed-pair constant source coherence (see the header comment of bandpassdata.jl for the model), then gauge-fix each (station, feed, time segment) track and write the result into the station blocks of the two observables (`writejointbandpass!`).

phase_blocks and amp_blocks are _bandpass_blocks(setup, θ, :phase) and (…, :logamp) — station blocks over the SAME θ this call is handed, since each block's θ is a view into it — phase_level_blocks/amp_level_blocks the matching _bandpass_level_blocks, empty without a level, and delay_blocks the phase's _bandpass_delay_blocks, empty without a delay. A station-uniform model gives one block per component spanning every station; where the model differs across stations, each block carries its own stations, feed tying, segments and prior, and a station no block covers is left at unit gain. Each track is fit under its own block's prior, which relates the values within each spectral window. A station's two shapes must hold the same stations on the same frequency and time segmentations, which validate_model(::JointSmoother, model) enforces at model-compile time. Either list may be empty, which holds that observable at zero: unit amplitude, or zero phase and no gauge.

geom supplies the stations the AntennaPair labels name (θ's stations, in θ's order) and the channel frequencies the priors are fit along.

Scaling a set of stations by one phase leaves every visibility internal to that set unchanged, so the data leave one phase free per such set: the connected components of the (station, feed, frequency segment, time segment) graph the correlations span (_joint_bandpass_gauge!). A pin holds the reference's slot in every component at zero, so the priors act on the other tracks relative to it. Under any other gauge the priors along frequency set every such shift but those the tracks' means take up — a constant under a level, a line under a delay — and only those are fixed, by a shift along them after the solve; where no prior acts, that is one per component.

scans is the per-scan (rl, wl) accumulator pairs from accumulate_bandpass — not summed across scans, since the source term needs each scan's own coherent visibility.

tseg, when given, is the (antenna, scan) time-segment table (_antenna_time_segments): station a's gain is solved separately for each distinct tseg[a, :] value, in that station's own segment numbering, and a station whose entry is 0 is left out of the solve. Every station shares one segment when it is omitted. scans must hold the scans tseg's columns describe, in the same order. The phase gauge acts on the graph these scans span, so a break at one station is measured against the stations that hold one gain across it rather than gauged away. Solved phases are comparable only WITHIN a component: where that graph splits — disjoint sub-arrays, or every station segmented at the same epoch — each piece carries its own arbitrary constant, and a per-station change read across the split is that constant plus the change.

The solve maximizes the posterior of the gains: χ²/2 of the visibilities with S at its closed-form optimum for the gains (profiled), plus each track's prior energy. S absorbs a constant on any (station, feed, time segment) track, which the data therefore cannot measure: a proper prior acting on the track holds it at the value the prior prefers, and on any other track one slot is held. The gauge's pins fix only what those holds leave of the shifts above, so the MAP is unique. The solve

  1. starts every slot at its own estimate given every other gain and S, repeated with S re-solved (StEFCal-style rounds), then searches each feed after the first for one delay common to every station against the products pairing it with an earlier feed, which the same-feed products the rounds follow do not see, and applies it where it is detected (_align_feed_delays!);
  2. seeds each prior's hyperparameters by type-II MAP on its track alone, and puts each phase track on the 2π branches most probable under it (_choose_branches!), once: the data are fringe-corrected, so phase steps along frequency are small;
  3. finds the MAP of the gains under the current hyperparameters by Levenberg–Marquardt on the profiled objective (_profiled_map!), each step solved by linsolve, a Krylov method of LinearSolve.jl, matrix-free and preconditioned by a sparse factorization (ProfilePreconditioner);
  4. fits the hyperparameters given as densities by MAP-EM, accelerated by SQUAREM (_squarem!): the E-step takes each track's state moments under the Gaussian posterior at the MAP, the M-step maximizes each track's expected log prior plus its hyperprior.

The MAP stops once the undamped step moves no gain by tolerance of its standard error from the data, once an accepted step lowers the objective by less than tolerance²/2, or once no damping lowers it, with a warning if that step would have moved a gain by tolerance or more. EM stops once a SQUAREM cycle moves no gain by tolerance of its standard error. Each warns when it runs out of iterations (max_iterations per MAP, max_em_iterations EM maps); an unconverged solve still depends on its starting point. The passes over the data run on executor. docs/src/priors.md ("The bandpass posterior") derives each step. The scans handed to one call are a connected piece of the coupling graph, so this is one criterion over one coupled problem: nodes that share no data are solved by separate calls rather than being averaged into a common tolerance.

Returns, per observable, one (; track, prior, status) per (station, feed, time segment) track: the track, the prior it was fit under with its hyperparameters resolved, and the TrackStatus of each spectral window. The data are reduced and the gains and source terms held in the accumulators' types, or in the real type eltype; the steps are solved in Float64.
